# Climate Factors: Rainfall, Temperature, and Ecosystem Stress

Rainfall variability, more than mean temperature, is typically the dominant climate constraint on species survival in semi-arid agro-ecosystems, since water availability directly limits both vegetation cover and soil moisture. IPCC assessments identify shifting rainfall patterns, including both prolonged droughts and intensified short-duration rainfall, as a primary mechanism by which climate change degrades terrestrial ecosystems, independent of temperature effects.

Temperature effects tend to act as threshold, rather than linear, stressors. Sustained temperatures above regional heat-stress thresholds reduce the active foraging window for pollinators and accelerate the decomposition (and therefore loss) of soil organic carbon, compounding degradation that started from a land-use or soil-management cause.

Because climate variables are largely outside a land manager's direct control at field scale, the most actionable response is to increase the resilience of the system to climate stress rather than to attempt to change the climate variables themselves. Restoring soil organic carbon and increasing vegetation cover both increase a plot's buffering capacity against rainfall variability and heat stress.

Source basis: IPCC AR6 WG1, Chapter 8: Water Cycle Changes (2021); IPCC AR6 WG2, Chapter 3: Terrestrial and Freshwater Ecosystems (2022); IPBES Global Assessment Report on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services (2019).
