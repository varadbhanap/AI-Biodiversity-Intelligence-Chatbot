# Human Impact: Pollution, Runoff, and Deforestation

Agrochemical runoff and industrial pollution load act primarily through soil and water pathways, suppressing microbial diversity and reducing pollinator survival even when other environmental metrics (soil organic carbon, land use) are otherwise favorable. This makes pollution load a metric that can silently cap the effectiveness of other interventions if left unaddressed.

Vegetated riparian buffer strips are the most evidence-backed intervention for reducing nutrient and agrochemical runoff into adjacent waterways, with field trials reporting nutrient runoff reductions in the range of 30-50%, alongside habitat corridor benefits from the added vegetation itself.

Deforestation in the surrounding landscape compounds these effects at a regional scale: forest loss increases habitat fragmentation directly and has been linked to reduced local rainfall through weakened regional moisture recycling. Assisted natural regeneration of degraded forest patches, which protects and lightly manages existing seed sources rather than replanting from scratch, has been shown to restore canopy cover measurably faster and at lower cost than active replanting programs.

Source basis: IPBES-IPCC Co-Sponsored Workshop Report on Biodiversity and Climate Change (2021); Stutter et al., Vegetated buffer strips and their function on agricultural runoff, Journal of Environmental Quality (2012); FAO, Global Forest Resources Assessment (2020); Chazdon & Guariguata, Natural regeneration as a tool for large-scale forest restoration, Biotropica (2016).
